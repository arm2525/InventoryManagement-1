Theory Essentials

Duration: 45 minutes References: Harris (1913); Wilson (1934); Hadley & Whitin (1963)

The Economic Order Quantity model minimizes the long-run average cost per unit time by balancing ordering and holding costs under deterministic demand. Harris (1913) first articulated the cost trade-off, while Wilson (1934) popularized the closed-form solution. The optimal order quantity is obtained by solving \min_{Q > 0} \{ KD Q + hQ 2 \} , where K is the fixed ordering cost, D the annual demand, and h the holding cost rate. Differentiation yields the well-known solution Q* = \(\sqrt{2KD/h}\).

Assumptions include stationary demand, instantaneous replenishment, and unlimited storage capacity. Hadley and Whitin (1963) provide a comprehensive treatment, including extensions for backorders and quantity discounts.

Workshop: EOQ Calibration and Sensitivity

  1. Estimate annual demand and holding cost from your research dataset.
  2. Compute EOQ, reorder cycle, and average inventory; document assumptions.
  3. Perform one-way sensitivity on K, D, and h and visualize the cost surface.
  4. Debate the validity of the deterministic assumption for your context.
Research prompt: Identify at least two papers extending EOQ to data-driven or stochastic environments (e.g., Chen et al., 2013). Summarize estimation techniques and discuss integration with your workshop findings.
