Theory Essentials

Duration: 45 minutes References: Arrow, Harris & Marschak (1951); Khouja (1999); Perakis & Roels (2007)

The single-period or newsvendor model selects an order quantity Q that maximizes expected profit under uncertain demand. Arrow, Harris, and Marschak (1951) derive the critical fractile condition \(F(Q^*) = \frac{p-c}{p-v}\), where p is the selling price, c the procurement cost, and v the salvage value. The ratio equates the marginal costs of overage and underage.

Behavioral and risk considerations lead to systematic deviations. Khouja (1999) surveys model variants (price-dependent demand, multiple products), while Perakis and Roels (2007) analyze robust newsvendor formulations with demand ambiguity sets.

Workshop: Critical Fractile Experiments

  1. Calibrate the profit-maximizing Q for three demand distributions (Normal, Poisson, empirical bootstrap).
  2. Simulate 5,000 trials to estimate expected profit and service level for each policy.
  3. Introduce risk-averse utility and recompute optimal Q; interpret changes.
  4. Draft discussion questions on behavioral biases (pull-to-center, demand chasing).
Research prompt: Identify empirical studies measuring real-world newsvendor performance (e.g., Schweitzer & Cachon, 2000). Design an experiment using Ph.D. cohorts to test decision support interventions.
