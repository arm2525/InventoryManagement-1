Theory Essentials

Duration: 45 minutes References: Zipkin (2000); Chopra & Meindl (2023); Graves (1986)

Safety stock protects against demand and supply variability. The classical approach computes the reorder point as R = dL + z\(\sigma_L\), where dL is mean demand over lead time, \(\sigma_L\) the lead-time demand standard deviation, and z the standard normal quantile achieving a target cycle-service level. Zipkin (2000) details how different service metrics (fill rate vs. CSL) lead to distinct formulations.

When lead times are stochastic or correlated, Graves (1986) shows that convolution methods and safety stock placement rules change materially. Chopra and Meindl (2023) extend the discussion to multi-echelon supply chains, emphasizing risk pooling and postponement.

Workshop: Service-Level Experimentation

  1. Derive demand and lead-time distributions from empirical data.
  2. Compute safety stock for CSL targets of 90%, 95%, and 99%; record resulting fill rates.
  3. Simulate 1,000 cycles to compare theoretical and simulated service levels.
  4. Design a policy memo recommending safety stock placement across two echelons.
