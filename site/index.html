Inventory Management Academy

A three-part graduate-level journey that builds from foundational theory to the latest research frontiers in inventory management. Each part is designed with rigorous theory, research references, and hands-on workshops tailored to Ph.D. students.

Part 1: Foundational Theory (3 hours) Part 2: Advanced Models (4.5 hours) Part 3: Research Trajectory (3 hours)

Program Structure

Part 1 — Foundations

Explore core models such as EOQ, safety stock, lot sizing, and single-period problems. Each module blends formal derivations, academic references, and interactive workshops with auto-graded quizzes and guided discussion prompts.

Duration: 3 hours
Deliverables: Completed workshop notebooks, quiz reflections, and annotated bibliography of seminal references.

Part 2 — Advanced Models

Extend foundational knowledge to stochastic control, service-level driven policies, multi-echelon systems, and data-driven methods. Emphasis on policy design under realistic constraints and integration with operations analytics.

Duration: 4.5 hours
Deliverables: Policy comparison memo, solved advanced workshop cases, and computational experiment logs.

Part 3 — Research Frontier

Trace the historical evolution of inventory research and synthesize current frontiers. Engage in literature discovery, research design critiques, and ideation of future research agendas.

Duration: 3 hours
Deliverables: Annotated timeline, research gap proposal, and peer discussion notes.

Learning Outcomes

  • Master analytical derivations and assumptions behind classical policies.
  • Translate theoretical models into computational tools for decision support.
  • Critically evaluate research contributions and identify open questions.
  • Design workshops that connect theory with empirical validation.

How to Use This Site

  1. Complete the modules in order; each builds on prior knowledge.
  2. Attempt quizzes before revealing hints; use results for self-assessment.
  3. Follow the research prompts to curate your own literature repository.
  4. Bring workshop artefacts to peer seminars for discussion.
Crafted for doctoral-level scholars in operations and supply chain management.
