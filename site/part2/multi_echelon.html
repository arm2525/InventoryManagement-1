Theory Essentials

Duration: 75 minutes References: Clark & Scarf (1960); Graves & Willems (2000); Chen & Federgruen (2000)

Clark and Scarf (1960) established echelon-base-stock optimality for serial systems by leveraging convexity and dynamic programming decomposition. Their result implies each stage follows a base-stock policy on echelon inventory. Graves and Willems (2000) introduced guaranteed-service models that decouple stages via lead-time reservations, enabling tractable optimization under service constraints.

For distribution systems, Chen and Federgruen (2000) analyzed multi-item joint replenishment and provided performance bounds for heuristic policies. Coordination contracts (buy-back, revenue sharing) align incentives across independent echelons.
