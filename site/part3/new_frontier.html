Theory Essentials: What is Being Researched *Today*?

Duration: 1.5 Hours References: See key references below

The "Classical Trajectory" module showed how problems were solved. This module discusses the problems that are *unsolved* or *newly created*. Modern research is defined by four major forces: (1) Human irrationality, (2) Environmental impact, (3) New retail channels, and (4) Global disruptions.

Stream 1: Behavioral Operations Management (BOM)

The "Golden Age" (Era 2) and "Supply Chain Era" (Era 4) assumed that all decision-makers were *rational* (i.e., they would *use* the optimal ($s, S$) policy or the channel-optimizing contract). Behavioral Operations, famously pioneered by Schweitzer & Cachon, challenges this.

  • Key Question: Why do real, educated managers *not* use the optimal models?
  • Seminal Finding (The Newsvendor Effect): When faced with a Newsvendor problem, real humans do *not* order the optimal $Q^*$. They exhibit systematic biases.
    • "Pull-to-Center": When $Q^*$ is high (high-profit items), managers order *too little*. When $Q^*$ is low (low-profit items), they order *too much*.
    • "Anchoring": Managers often anchor their decision on the *mean demand* $\mu$, failing to properly account for the $C_u$ and $C_o$ costs.
  • Frontier: How do we design *debiasing* contracts or *decision-support systems* (e.g., AI) that correct for this predictable human bias?

Stream 2: Sustainability & Closed-Loop Supply Chains

Era 1-4 models assumed a "forward" linear flow: Supplier → Firm → Customer. This stream introduces a "reverse" flow, driven by environmental and economic pressures.

  • Key Question: How do we manage inventory in a system where products are *remanufactured* or *recycled*?
  • New Models (Closed-Loop Supply Chains): These models are far more complex. A firm now has to manage multiple, interacting inventory stocks:
    1. Inventory of *new* products.
    2. Inventory of *returned cores* (used products).
    3. Inventory of *remanufactured* products.
  • Frontier: What is the optimal inventory policy when a *carbon cap* or *carbon tax* is introduced? This creates a multi-objective optimization problem: $\min \text{Cost}(Q)$ s.t. $\text{Carbon}(Q) \le \text{Cap}$.

Stream 3: Omnichannel & On-Demand Economy

The rise of e-commerce and the "gig economy" has shattered the classic multi-echelon structure.

  • Key Question: What is the optimal inventory policy when a retail store is *also* a mini-warehouse for online orders (BOPIS: Buy-Online-Pickup-In-Store)?
  • New Models (Store-as-Warehouse): This creates a conflict. An item in a store now has two competing sources of demand:
    1. A physical customer (who gets angry if it's not on the shelf).
    2. An online customer (who expects it to be available for shipping).
  • Frontier: How do you model inventory for the **On-Demand Economy** (e.g., Uber Eats, DoorDash)? The "inventory" (e.g., of food, or delivery drivers) is mobile, perishable, and must be positioned *proactively* based on ML forecasts.

Stream 4: Supply Chain Resilience & Disruption

This field, which was niche for 20 years, became the most important topic in operations post-2020. The classic models (EOQ, etc.) are $\min \text{Cost}$ models, which prioritize *efficiency*. Resilience models prioritize *robustness*.

  • Key Question: How much "unproductive" inventory should we hold to protect against a low-probability, high-impact disruption (e.g., a factory fire, a port closure, a pandemic)?
  • New Models (Dual-Sourcing, etc.): This research models the trade-off between a cheap, unreliable supplier and an expensive, reliable one. It often uses game theory or MDPs.
  • Frontier: How do we move beyond "cost" as the only metric? Researchers are optimizing for *new* metrics, like **Time-to-Recover (TTR)** or the **Probability of Survival**. This is a central theme in modern Ph.D. research.

Workshop: Literature Stream Analysis

  1. Activity: In groups, select *one* of the four research streams above.
  2. Using Google Scholar or Scopus, find 3-5 papers on that topic published in a top journal (e.g., *Management Science*, *Operations Research*, *M&SOM*) in the *last 5 years*.
  3. Prepare a short presentation answering:
    1. What is the specific research question (RQ) they address?
    2. What is their primary methodology (e.g., Analytical Model, Game Theory, Simulation, Empirical Data)?
    3. What is their key "contribution" or "finding"?
Research prompt: Based on your literature stream analysis, identify a "gap." What is the *next* logical research question that is still unanswered? This is the first step of your own research proposal.
